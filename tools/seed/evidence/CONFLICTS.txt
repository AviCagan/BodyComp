back/vertical_pull_pronated: Anchor Wide-Grip_Lat_Pulldown credits upper_back at 0.5, yet lower trapezius reaches 45-56% MVIC in pull-ups (Youdas 2010) and middle trapezius ~48% MVIC in pronated pull-ups (Dickie 2017), which is close to the 60% threshold usually treated as a primary-mover level; 0.75 would be defensible for the hanging pull-up variants. Anchor kept; only the region split was biased toward traps_mid_lower.
back/vertical_pull_pronated: Anchor biceps 0.5 for the pulldown is well supported for the pronated machine pulldown (biceps <20% MVIC, Borms 2016) but hanging pull-ups reach 78-96% MVIC biceps (Youdas 2010) and pulldown-only training grew elbow flexors as much as adding curls (Gentil 2013); a 0.75 credit for hanging pull-ups would be defensible. Kept at 0.5 for consistency with the anchor.
back/vertical_pull_supinated_neutral: The brief's half-credit default for synergists gives chin-ups biceps 0.5 by analogy to the pulldown anchor, but supinated hanging pulls reach 78-96% MVIC biceps (Youdas 2010) and pulldown training alone matches curl-added training for elbow-flexor growth (Gentil 2013). We used 0.75 for supinated variants within the tunable 0.25-1.0 range; the pulldown anchor itself is untouched.
back/vertical_pull_supinated_neutral: No anchor credits chest for any pulling movement, yet pectoralis major reaches 44-57% MVIC in chin-ups (Youdas 2010) and is consistently higher in close/supinated/front-path pulldowns than behind the neck (Signorile 2002; Sperandei 2009). We added chest 0.25 (lower-biased) to supinated/neutral vertical pulls; if the product prefers pulls never to colour the chest, drop this row.
back/straight_arm_pulldown: No conflict with an anchor. Note that the current app mapping credits biceps 0.5 for straight-arm pulldowns; there is no elbow flexion in the movement, so this was removed.
back/bent_over_row_free: Anchor Bent_Over_Barbell_Row erectors 0.25: the standing bent-over row produces the highest lumbar erector activation and spine load of the row family (Fenwick 2009), well above bench-supported rows (Garcia-Jaen 2021), and the Barbell_Squat anchor credits erectors 0.5 for a comparable isometric trunk-extension demand. 0.5 would be internally consistent; anchor kept at 0.25.
back/bent_over_row_free: Anchor lats 0.75 for rows: EMG comparisons find latissimus activation in bent-over/seated/inverted rows statistically equal to the lat pulldown (Edelburg/Porcari 2018; Di Fonza 2026). The brief's row/pulldown asymmetry is a product decision rather than an EMG finding; we kept it and only promoted lats to 1.0 for close-neutral-grip and supinated variants where elbow-tucked mechanics bias toward the lat.
back/supported_row: Delt_rear 0.5 for rows vs 0.25 for pulldowns: Franke 2015 found posterior deltoid activation did not differ between the seated row and an inclined pulldown (both well below the reverse pec deck), so the 2x gap between the row and pulldown anchors is not supported by that study; anchors kept.
back/one_arm_row: The bent-over row anchor places upper_back above lats (1.0/0.75); this family inverts that (lats 1.0, upper_back 0.75) for elbow-tucked unilateral rows. That is a calibration choice from mechanics (Di Fonza 2026) and practitioner consensus, not a contradiction of a fixed anchor row.
chest/flat_barbell_bench_press: delt_front 0.5 may under-credit the front delt: Lanza 2024 (MRI) found anterior deltoid CSA +15% vs pec +20% after bench-only training, i.e. ~75% of the primary mover's growth; 0.75 would be defensible. Anchor kept.
chest/flat_barbell_bench_press: Chest split {.25/.50/.25}: Rodriguez-Ridao 2020 shows equal EMG across all three portions at 0 deg, so as an activation statement the split would be ~{.33/.33/.33}; Chaves 2020 hypertrophy supports upper being the smallest share. Anchor kept (emphasis, not absolute).
chest/incline_press: delt_front 0.5: at 45 deg and above the anterior deltoid EMG equals or exceeds the clavicular pec (Rodriguez-Ridao 2020: AD ~33% vs PMUP ~30% MVIC; Trebs 2010; Barnett 1995), so 0.75 would be justified for steep inclines. Anchor kept.
chest/incline_press: chest_lower 0.05: Lauver 2016 still recorded ~72% MVIC in the lower pec at 45 deg and Chaves 2020 found similar 5th-intercostal growth in the incline group, so the costal region is not unloaded; 0.05 is acceptable only as an emphasis share, not as activation. Anchor kept.
core/sit_up: In a full sit-up the hip flexors are a co-prime mover for the second half of the range (Juker 1998; Andersson 1997), so 0.5 under-represents them relative to the brief's 'primary mover = 1.0' rule; kept at 0.5 because the brief treats hip_flexors as an optional 0.5-multiplier group and users log sit-ups as ab work.
core/reverse_crunch_leg_tuck: For straight-leg and seated-tuck variants the hip flexors are arguably the prime mover (the abdominals mostly work isometrically to hold the pelvis); the brief's half-credit rule caps them below 1.0. Set to 0.75 as the strongest allowed synergist tier rather than 1.0.
core/hanging_leg_raise: Straight-leg hanging raises without a pelvic curl are hip-flexor dominant; the brief's secondary cap keeps hip_flexors at 0.75 where a strict prime-mover reading might give 1.0.
core/hanging_leg_raise: Forearm credit for the bar hang is an isometric-stabiliser credit that the brief discourages; included at the 0.25 floor because grip is commonly the limiting factor, flagged for the team to drop if unwanted.
core/anti_extension_plank_rollout: Planks are entirely isometric; the brief's rule against crediting isometric stabilisers cannot apply to the target muscle, so abs/obliques are credited on the EMG magnitude (RA ~45%, EO 45-77% MVIC).
core/trunk_rotation_anti_rotation: Pallof press is a pure isometric; the brief's isometric-stabiliser exclusion cannot apply to the target muscles, so obliques get 1.0 on the anti-rotation demand itself.
core/lateral_flexion_side_bend: Quadratus lumborum has no group; its work in side bends and side bridges is credited to erectors at 0.25-0.5 as the nearest visible group, per the brief's proxy instruction.
core/lateral_flexion_side_bend: Gluteus medius credit in the side bridge/side jackknife is an isometric-stabiliser credit; included at 0.25 because 74% MVIC exceeds the strengthening threshold used in the source study.
core/balance_board_ankle_stability: Balance-board work does not produce a hypertrophic stimulus comparable to a hard set; counting it as 1.0 calf sets inflates calf coverage. Recommend either excluding pattern 'other' balance drills from volume scoring or applying a low hardness so it colours without counting.
hinge_posterior/conventional_deadlift: The brief says not to credit muscles that only stabilise isometrically; forearms (grip), lats, and upper back in the deadlift are largely isometric, yet practitioner consensus and the loads involved argue for colour. Kept forearms/upper_back/traps_upper at 0.5 and lats at 0.25 as an explicit exception; flag if the product wants isometric work zeroed.
small_groups/seated_calf_raise: Kinoshita 2023 measured essentially zero gastrocnemius growth from seated raises (0.6%/1.7%, not significant), which argues the anchor's 0.2 gastrocnemius share is generous; 0.1/0.9 would fit the data better. Anchor kept as instructed.
triceps/cable_pushdown: Maeo 2023 shows the overhead vs neutral arm position changes TOTAL triceps growth by ~1.4x in every head, with almost no change in the long:monoarticular ratio. The brief's anchors (overhead .7/.3 vs bench .3/.7) therefore express a head-emphasis contrast that the hypertrophy data only weakly support, and the weight cap of 1.0 cannot express that overhead work delivers more stimulus per set than pushdowns. Anchors left untouched; pushdown set to .4/.6 rather than .3/.7 as a partial correction.
triceps/cable_pushdown: Kholinne 2018 (EMG/modelling) found the long head MOST active with the arm at the side (0 deg elevation) and the medial head dominant overhead, the opposite of the practitioner consensus encoded in the anchors. Hypertrophy data (Maeo, Brandao) are weighted above it, but it is a genuine tier-2 conflict.
triceps/overhead_extension: Kholinne 2018 reports the MEDIAL head taking over at >=90 deg shoulder elevation (EMG + OpenSim), which contradicts the .7 long-head share of the anchor. Hypertrophy data (Maeo 2023, Brandao 2020) support the anchor and are weighted higher.
triceps/close_grip_bench_press: Saeterbakken 2017 and Tanimoto 2023 suggest triceps and pec activation barely change with grip width, which argues the close-grip bench is closer to the bench anchor (chest 1.0 / triceps 0.5) than to a triceps-primary lift. The triceps-primary mapping is kept because the triceps limit the load (6RM 6-11% lower) and the app's users expect close-grip work to colour triceps first; chest is set to 0.75 rather than 0.5 to reflect the evidence.
triceps/partial_rom_lockout_press: The bench anchor gives chest 1.0 / triceps 0.5; making the partial-ROM versions triceps 1.0 / chest 0.75 assumes the missing bottom range is worth more to the pecs than to the triceps. Kassiano 2023 and Fischer 2025 support this direction, but there is no longitudinal study of board/floor presses; if the team prefers, chest 1.0 / triceps 0.75 is an equally defensible alternative.
